Inferno

Dan Brown

Read: April 2, 2026 • Rating: 8/10

Have you ever thought about overpopulation? Is this a global problem? Can this be a reason that humans can go extinct one day? I hadn't had any idea before reading this book. I mean, this makes sense. Although the birth rate is steadily declining right now, people are living much more than they used to because of better quality of life.

The book talks a lot about denial. The concept of "denial" is interesting. For example, when you see something horrible that is happening to someone, something, anything, and you ignore it, that's called denial. Yeah, it won't hurt you. Yeah, you can have your own reasons for saving yourself or not minding others' business. But it tests your humanity. In Uzbek we have a word "odamgarchilik" that can describe it perfectly. Helping each other, using our emotional intelligence to fight against denial is odamgarchilik in some sense. Because we are not built to live all alone or do everything for ourselves all the time. We are built to live in companionship, in a group to make each others' lives a little easier. And these acts of service humble us. They humble our ever-lived ego.

I like how Dan Brown interconnects past and present. By past, I mean the history behind myths, cities, countries, symbols. I always find mysteries in commonly-known cities. Interestingly, all the events in this single thick book happen only in 24 hours. Can you imagine? I always get fascinated how Dan Brown can hold the intrigue throughout the book and open its secrets one by one like solving a puzzle. Unless you read his books till the end, you would never get the full picture of what had actually happened to the characters.

The darkest places in hell are reserved for those who maintain neutrality in times of moral crisis. In dangerous times, there is no sin greater than inaction... When it came to circumstances of the world, denial had become a global pandemic.
