1. Context

Makon means “a place to live.” It is a web studio for redesigning a room: you photograph one room, and Makon keeps its walls, windows and light while redesigning what sits inside.

It doesn't stop at the picture. You can build the room yourself as a measured floor plan, walk through it in 3D, design and place every piece of furniture at its real size, and finish with a brief you can print and hand to whoever is doing the work.

2. Problem and Objective

An AI render on its own is just a picture. It looks good, but it doesn't tell you how many litres of paint to buy, whether the sofa fits, or whether you can still open the door.

Makon was built to close that gap:

  • Keep the real room (walls, windows, daylight) and only change what you choose
  • Turn the idea into measurements, quantities and a budget
  • Let anyone build their room in 3D and furnish it piece by piece, with or without a photo

3. How It Works

One project moves through five tools. Each one hands its result to the next.

4. Keep the Room, Change the Style

The redesign has to look like your room. So you choose what stays exactly as it is: the window plane, doors and archways, floor, ceiling and lights, wall colour, built-in units, radiators or the furniture layout. You also set how strong the change is and which way the daylight comes from.

Your request always comes first: if you write “keep my green sofa”, that wins over the style and the keep options. A style is the starting point, not a filter. It carries a palette, finishes for the 3D room and a materials list for the brief. Pick one:

Renders are kept as a version history (up to 24), and you can refine one in plain words (“make the rug lighter”) with undo. The palette is extracted from every render, with copyable colour codes. Any redesign can be shared as a branded before/after picture or a 9:16 reel, ready for Instagram.

5. Build Your Own Room

You don't need a photo to start. Makon has a full floor-plan editor: begin from a rectangle or an L-shape, or draw every wall yourself with corner, right-angle and alignment snapping. Doors come with a real swing and a hinge side you can flip; windows get a width, a height and a sill. Set the ceiling height, work in metric or imperial, and measure one wall with a tape so the whole plan calibrates to it.

On a phone the same editor works with your fingers: pinch to zoom, drag to pan, tap to select. Every change can be undone.

6. Walk Through It in 3D

The 3D view is built from the plan with three.js, with real wall openings, glass, doors, skirting, and plank or tile floors. It isn't a diagram. Upholstery has rounded cushions, wood, metal and fabric each have their own material, and soft reflections make them read as real.

  • Six ways to look: perspective, top, front, side, eye level and a slow turntable
  • Day and evening: the sun follows the daylight direction you set, with soft shadows; in the evening the room darkens and the lamps glow
  • Move things in 3D: click a piece and drag it across the floor, and the plan updates with it
  • From 3D back to AI: “Use this view in Design” sends the 3D view to the AI as the room photo, so a room you built from nothing can become a realistic picture

7. Design Every Piece

Furnishing works like a real shop floor. A picture catalogue of 47 pieces across 9 groups (seating, tables, storage, beds, lighting, decor, kitchen, bath and office) shows each piece as the same 3D model you'll see in the room. Add one and it finds a free spot by itself.

Then every piece is yours to shape: its name, width, depth and height to the centimetre, rotation to the degree, its colour, and how high it hangs if it goes on a wall. Duplicate it, rotate it 90°, or remove it.

Furniture is checked as you place it: through a wall, outside the room, overlapping, blocking a door swing, or leaving a walkway under 60 cm. Try the walkway rule:

Type a price next to any piece and it goes straight into the budget in the build brief.

8. Start From a Ready Room

For people who would rather start from something beautiful, Makon has 12 ready-made rooms, from a warm living room and a Japandi studio flat to a kids' room and a Mediterranean bathroom. Three premium dark rooms (a Noir bedroom, an Obsidian lounge and an Umber kitchen-living) are among them. Each one is a real measured plan with real furniture, and it opens as your own copy to change.

Alongside them are signature interiors: original photographs and short films of rooms by Makon's founder, each with its colours and materials listed so you can bring the look home.

9. System Architecture

Makon is a static site with one serverless function. Almost everything runs in the browser as plain JavaScript modules, with no framework and no build step.

10. Engineering Decisions

Most of the work went into making it dependable for people I'll never meet.

  • No silent fallback. If an AI render fails, Makon says why instead of quietly showing something else. The on-device colour preview is offered only as itself, labelled honestly
  • Start without sign-up. Anyone can open the Studio and start designing straight away, with no account needed first
  • A free path and a paid path. Cloudflare Workers AI (about 100 renders a day, free) or OpenAI for higher quality. If both are set, OpenAI is used
  • Fair limits. Each visitor gets 5 AI renders a day by default, and failed renders don't count. Upstash Redis makes the limit exact, an owner code skips it, and an optional access code locks rendering to people I share it with
  • Errors without secrets. Provider errors are made readable without ever echoing key text back to the browser
  • No CDN dependency. three.js is bundled and served with long-lived immutable caching
  • Data stays local, but swappable. Accounts and storage live behind two small modules, so a backend like Supabase can replace them without touching the rest

11. What This Project Demonstrates

Makon is product engineering end to end: an AI pipeline that puts the person's request first and is honest when it fails, a geometry-aware 2D editor, a real-time 3D scene with its own furniture library, and the unglamorous parts that make a tool usable, like undo, autosave, exports, limits and a phone layout with a bottom tab bar.
