Why I'm Building Makon

October 27, 2026·485 words

(Makon means “a place to live.” That's the whole idea, really.)

Let me explain why I'm building it.

Most people will never hire an interior designer. Not because they don't care about where they live, but because it's expensive, it feels like something for other people, and honestly, most of us don't even know where to start. So we live with the room we have. We move the sofa once, maybe buy a new lamp, and that's it.

But almost everyone has stood in their own room at some point and wondered what it could be. I think that small moment deserves an answer.

Now here's the interesting part: AI can already make beautiful pictures of rooms. But the problem is, a picture is just a picture. It looks good, but it doesn't tell you whether the sofa actually fits, whether you can still open the door, or how much paint to buy. (And sometimes it quietly changes your window, which is not very helpful when you can't move your window.)

So I wanted something that starts from the real room and ends with something you can actually do.

That's why in Makon, your words come first. If you say “keep my green sofa,” it keeps your green sofa, whatever style you picked. Your walls, windows and light stay. Then the idea doesn't stop at the picture: you can build the room on a measured plan, walk through it in 3D, choose every piece of furniture and change its size to the centimetre, and Makon checks that nothing blocks a door or a walkway. At the end you get a brief with materials, quantities and a budget.

And if you don't have a photo, that's fine too. You can just build the room yourself, from the walls up.

There's one rule I hold myself to while building it: be honest. If the AI fails, Makon says why. It doesn't hand you something else and pretend. I think a tool that people trust with their home has to earn that trust in small things first.

I've also noticed something about myself: I care a lot about how things look and feel. Rooms change how we think, how we rest, even how we treat the people inside them. A calm room makes a calmer person (at least it does for me). So making that easier for more people feels like something worth spending my time on.

Is Makon finished? No, not even close. Every week I find something to make clearer, faster or more honest. But it already does the thing I wanted most: it lets anyone see what their own place could become, before spending a single som.

That's why I'm building it. If you want to try it, you don't even need to sign up: makon-khaki.vercel.app. And if you want to see how it's made, the whole story is here.
