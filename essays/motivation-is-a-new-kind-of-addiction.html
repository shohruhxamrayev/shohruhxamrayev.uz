Motivation Is a New Kind of Addiction

March 23, 2026 • 2 min read

You save hundreds of motivational videos every day, follow countless successful people, and yet nothing in your life changes. Why? Because your brain can't tell the difference between planning to do something and actually doing it.

When you watch a 5-minute video about waking up early, your brain releases dopamine as if you'd actually done it yourself. As a result, the need to take real action disappears.

The algorithm exploits your weakness. Social media platforms (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube) make money off of you. To them, it's far more profitable for you to endlessly watch videos about success than to actually achieve it. Because someone who's reached their goal puts the phone down and gets to work. But someone who's still chasing that goal keeps scrolling forever.

Be honest with yourself. If your current peaceful life and rest actually matter more to you, it's better to admit that. Don't torture yourself with fake motivation.

You're not actually afraid of failure. What you're afraid of is discovering that you don't actually want to suffer for success. You've trained your brain to believe the lie that "watching is the same as participating." You need to stop collecting information and start taking real action, starting today.
